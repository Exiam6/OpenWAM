## Question and research question it serves

Research question (user's words, 2026-10-02): can the RAE representation-autoencoder ideas raise the OpenWAM policy's RoboTwin closed-loop success?

This experiment answers, yes / no / by how much, for the 768-dim DINOv3 ("raw768") route:

- **H1** Does changing the training video-noise shift from 5 to 16.4317 (RAE prescription for m = 1,105,920 nominal dims), with the native head, change paired fresh-scene success?
- **H2** Does the RAE wide head (3072 / 24 heads / depth 1 / MLP 2), at shift 5, change paired fresh-scene success? (Replication on fresh scenes of the ex2 reading 24/60 vs 32/60 on adjust_bottle.)
- **H3** Is there an interaction: wide head at shift 16.4317 vs native head at shift 16.4317 (H3a), and shift 16.4317 vs 5 under the wide head (H3b)?

What it cannot answer: whether these changes help a Wan-VAE latent policy (no VAE arm), whether they help at official scale (20k steps, 765 demos, 12-layer 1024-dim DiT only), and anything about the two tasks that were at zero for every arm in ex2 if they stay at zero here (then only adjust_bottle carries information).

## Hypotheses and competing explanations

- H1 (shift): RAE's shift mechanism says high-dimensional latents need more noise during training; the prescription for our latent is 16.43. Competing explanation: the effective dimension of DINOv3 features is far below nominal, so 5 is already adequate and 16.43 only wastes training on near-pure-noise samples. The corrected sigma-curve audit of 2026-10-10 (unweighted MSE ratio 0.997–1.022 across σ) already suggests the two settings fit equally; closed-loop success decides.
- H2 (wide head): a 3072-wide shallow head relieves the 1024-dim trunk bottleneck for 3072-dim patches. Competing explanation: the head fits the video target (dev video loss 3.6× lower in ex1) without changing the action stream, so success is unchanged or lower (ex2: lower).
- H3 (interaction): the RAE paper combines both; either may need the other. The 2×2 design separates them: H3a/H3b are single-variable contrasts at the other factor's new level.
